## 1. Prioritization Framework Comparative Analysis
To select the optimal mathematical formulation for the AI Workspace, four mainstream task prioritization paradigms were evaluated:

### 1.1 The Classical Eisenhower Matrix
- Segregates tasks into four quadrants based on binary combinations of Urgency and Importance.
- **Limitation:** Fails to account for estimated duration or calendar availability. A task that takes 5 minutes vs. 8 hours is treated identically.

### 1.2 Weighted Shortest Job First (WSJF)
- Commonly used in Agile scheduling: $\text{WSJF} = \frac{\text{Cost of Delay}}{\text{Duration}}$.
- **Limitation:** Tends to over-prioritize trivial short tasks while starving long-duration high-impact academic deadlines.

### 1.3 Musthafa & Muthukumaran Urgency-Decay Model (2024)
- Formulates priority as a continuous non-linear decay function:
  $$P = w_1 \cdot e^{-\lambda t_{\text{deadline}}} + w_2 \cdot I_{\text{user}} + w_3 \cdot D_{\text{deficit}} + w_4 \cdot W_{\text{workload}}$$
- **Verdict:** Selected as the ground-truth formulation for AI Workspace because it simultaneously factors in deadline proximity, unbooked calendar minutes, and user importance.

## 2. Validation of Synthetic Task Dataset
The synthetic dataset generated by Member 1 (`ml/data/synthetic_tasks.csv`, $N = 5,000$ samples) was subjected to rigorous exploratory data analysis and validation:

1. **Schema Completeness:** 100% complete records. Zero missing (`NaN`) values detected across all 14 columns.
2. **Distribution Balance:**
   - Medium Priority: $48.3\%$ ($2,413$ samples)
   - Low Priority: $30.8\%$ ($1,539$ samples)
   - High Priority: $21.0\%$ ($1,048$ samples)
   - Accurately mirrors real-world student/professional workload distributions where critical deadlines constitute ~20-25% of all commitments.
3. **Overdue Edge Case Integrity:** Overdue tasks ($8.2\%$ of samples, $h < 0$) are mapped unconditionally to High Priority, verifying that severe negative slack time cannot be misclassified as low priority.
4. **Leakage Verification:** Target priority labels were strictly isolated during feature scaling and train/test splitting.
